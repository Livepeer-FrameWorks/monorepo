---
title: "Test and trial accounts"
description: "Grant temporary billing arrangements and revoke account access."
---

Create a tenant through signup or bootstrap, then grant a trial as a platform
operator:

```bash
frameworks admin billing grant set --email trial@example.com --tier enterprise \
  --base-fee 0 --waive-usage --until 2026-12-31 --reason "Enterprise evaluation"
frameworks admin billing grant list --expiring 7d
frameworks admin billing grant show --email trial@example.com
frameworks admin billing grant revoke --email trial@example.com --reason "Evaluation complete"
```

A date includes that UTC day; an RFC3339 timestamp names an exact deadline.
`--fallback-tier` overrides the original tier restored when the grant ends.
Renewing a grant preserves its original fallback, including when `--tier` is
omitted or unchanged. An explicit `admin billing set-tier` clears that
fallback and preserves the billing arrangement on the assigned tier until it
ends.

A minute sweep closes an expired grant and restores its fallback. A trial
returning to zero-balance PAYG cannot ingest or play media until funded. A
grant without fallback leaves the current tier in place; collection readiness
continues to gate rated API work. Existing postpaid media admission is
unchanged. The free tier requires no payment provider. Trial expiry blocks new
ingest but does not proactively end an existing positive-balance publisher;
prepaid suspension stops it once its balance becomes negative.

Renewing a deadline or re-sending the same prices keeps the current billing
period open. Tenants cannot change tier themselves while a grant is
outstanding, and scheduled downgrades wait until it ends. An operator can use
`set-tier` to change the granted tier through a billing boundary. Complete or
reconcile any pending provider checkout before setting a grant. The refusal
identifies the checkout deadline; it is not a deadline for the grant. A
completed checkout keeps its reservation while payment settles, even after the
checkout deadline. Inspect outstanding work and recover an abandoned checkout:

```bash
frameworks admin billing recovery list --tenant-id <uuid>
frameworks admin billing recovery checkout --tenant-id <uuid> --reservation-id <uuid> --reason "Abandoned evaluation checkout"
frameworks admin billing recovery list --tenant-id <uuid> --history
```

Recovery displays the provider, state and checkout amount before asking for
confirmation. Use `--yes` to confirm without prompting. It reports whether the
checkout expired, the subscription activated, cancellation/refund
reconciliation finished, or work remains pending. An accepted refund request
can still await bank settlement. Refund work remains visible in `recovery list`
until Stripe or Mollie confirms the returned amount; a failed bank return raises
`PurserProviderWebhookInboxStuck`. Inspect the exact provider payment and repair
the missing return in that provider's console. Settlement polling only reads
provider receipts and never sends another refund. For cluster purchases, only
the initial unactivated purchase is returned; its corresponding operator credit
is voided without reversing credit for delivered recurring service. Every request retains its operator identity,
reason and outcome in history and emits audit events; a later request does not
overwrite it.

An on-time completed purchase is reconciled into its subscription when
settlement succeeds. Failed settlement releases its reservation. An abandoned
or unbound attempt can be recovered before its deadline, while a completed
purchase still settling remains reserved. Failed recovery stays durable.
Historical Mollie receipts without a unique funding link require local activation
evidence before their initial collection can be treated as delivered service.
Ambiguous history stays pending with the exact subscription and payment in its
diagnostic; verify these in the provider console before reconciling them.
Normal settlement waits back off from one minute to six hours and keep their
reason visible; waits exceeding fourteen days raise
`PurserProviderWebhookInboxStuck`.

Grants change prices prospectively. Closing documents preserve charges before
the grant and waive only the granted phase. Ended and replaced grants remain
available with `grant list --include-expired` for audit and historical
pricing. An existing Stripe or Mollie subscription must be cancelled before a
base-fee override.

Gateway billing caches may take about two minutes to refresh. Signed media
permission is bounded by the trial deadline, including while a media cell is
disconnected. Early revocation reaches connected cells through authority
refresh; a disconnected cell retains already-issued permission until its
signed expiry.

For reversible user suspension:

```bash
frameworks admin users deactivate --email trial@example.com --reason "Evaluation paused"
frameworks admin users activate --email trial@example.com --reason "Evaluation resumed"
```

Deactivation ends existing interactive sessions, API-token WebSockets, and
stream-key publishing. Tenant-owned streams and VODs keep their playback
availability. Activation requires a fresh interactive login; previously
revoked sessions do not return. Connected cells receive stream-key revocation
and an active-ingest stop through the durable refresh queue. Once received,
they enforce it even while Commodore is unavailable. Unreachable cells retry
and raise `CommodoreAccountIngestRevocationStuck`. A reactivation supersedes
queued stops; a stop already in flight can end a newly connected publisher
once. Reconnect after reactivation if that happens. Billing restrictions still
apply to API work, ingest and playback.

`frameworks admin tenants deactivate <UUID> --yes --tenant-name "Exact Tenant
Name" --reason "Account closed"` requires a matching Quartermaster tenant name.
Omit both confirmation flags to type the name interactively; `--tenant-name`
requires `--yes`. Closure permanently closes all users and queues a durable
stop for their active ingest. Quartermaster teardown proceeds independently;
unreachable cells retry and raise the `CommodoreAccountIngestRevocationStuck` alert. The alert
continues until the cell is restored. Confirming that a cell is permanently
offline does not clear it: no cluster-retirement command exists. This alert is aggregated and has no cell label: silencing it suppresses all
account-ingest revocation stalls. Record permanent removals in the incident;
restoring the cell is currently the only way to complete its stop obligation.
Foghorn v0.3.13 returns Unimplemented for per-user stops during a rolling
upgrade; those stops retry until its Foghorn is upgraded. Closure publishes
tenant-inactive authority without authorizing data deletion. It cannot be reversed. Remove closed users from bootstrap declarations before the next release apply;
otherwise validation refuses their reactivation. The local closure
blocks new active users even if Quartermaster is temporarily unavailable.

Purser consumes the permanent closure event and durably records its billing
cutoff. It cancels established Stripe and Mollie subscriptions without refunding
completed service, then finalizes the open billing phase at that cutoff. No
later recurring invoice is started. Provider outages and incomplete metering
keep the finalization pending; inspect `frameworks admin billing recovery list
--tenant-id <uuid>`. An outstanding grant first closes at the cutoff through
the normal expiry worker. Late paid or settling cluster callbacks queue provider
cancellation, including after final billing; they cannot restore access or
finalize another billing document.

Open cluster checkouts are expired so they cannot accept another purchase.
Activated paid cluster subscriptions are cancelled without refunding or crediting their unused
paid time. A paid tenant or cluster checkout that never activated is refunded
only for its checkout payment or first invoice; recurring service payments are
never part of checkout compensation.
Normal subscription cancellation stops renewal at the paid-through date; permanent
account closure ends access immediately. Failed or duplicate checkout recovery is
a separate compensation action, not a refund policy for ordinary cancellation.

`PurserOperatorGrantExpiryStuck` exposes its last error and failed-attempt count through `grant show` and `grant list
--include-expired`. Commodore synchronization failures raise
`CommodoreTenantAccountClosureStuck` independently of billing finalization.

For an operator-owned account, declare a Quartermaster tenant, a Purser
`customer_billing` row with `model: postpaid`, `tier: enterprise`, and
`operator_grant: {base_fee: "0", waive_usage: true, reason: "Operator test
account"}`, then declare its users in `accounts`. Put passwords in the normal
SOPS secret references. Release apply reconciles accounts after Purser without
resetting existing passwords. No grant expiry means the arrangement lasts
until revoked. A live declaration owns the grant and logs any CLI arrangement
it replaces. An expired declaration is inert and leaves later CLI grants
alone. Remove a live declaration before a permanent CLI revocation.

Failed expiry reconciliation is recorded with five-minute backoff and alerted;
later expiries continue. Correct the underlying billing or pricing error;
retries run automatically.

Bridge closes API-token WebSockets on the live revocation event. A replica
that missed the event rechecks `ValidateAPIToken` every minute, bounding the
remaining socket access.

Release apply attempts pending grant expiry before reconciling a live billing
declaration. Dry-run leaves pending expiries untouched and reports those
tenants as deferred.

Read private deactivation and closure reasons with `frameworks admin tenants
history <UUID>`. Only platform operators can read this audit.

If bootstrap defers a tenant, healthy tenants commit and the command exits
non-zero, stopping release apply before the accounts step. Fix the reported
failure and re-run release apply. When expiry restored a different billing
model, run `frameworks admin billing set-tier` with the declared tier,
`--billing-model` and a reason before retrying, so the normal transition
handles balances and credits. An obsolete fallback can be recovered with
`grant revoke --tier <active-tier>` or an explicit `set-tier`; both close the
expired grant at its deadline.

An expired grant awaiting reconciliation must be ended with `grant revoke
--tier` or `set-tier` before setting another grant. A rejected replacement
leaves the original grant and billing phase unchanged. Cancelling a
subscription keeps its grant so recoverable provider states such as Stripe
unpaid do not lose waived usage. Revoke or expiry on a cancelled subscription
archives the grant at the cleanup time without changing tier or issuing a
closing document. Revoke it before starting a new provider checkout. Renewal
of the same established Stripe subscription may restore status and dates while
retaining the grant; a new checkout or activation must end it first.

Checkout reservations have a deadline and belong to one provider attempt.
Failures before checkout creation and definite provider rejections release the
reservation immediately. Ambiguous results remain reserved until the durable
provider inbox reconciles them after the checkout deadline;
`PurserProviderWebhookInboxStuck` covers provider outages or compensation
failures. Historical receipts already installed as the tenant's subscription
do not block grants. Other unresolved receipts are queued for recovery before
they release their reservation. A provider activation awaiting complete usage
metering keeps the first webhook receipt as its billing boundary. An open
metering anomaly holds that activation; `PurserProviderWebhookInboxStuck`
covers retries while the anomaly is resolved.

A timed-out create retries with its original parameters and idempotency key.
Replay is limited to 23 hours for Stripe and 45 minutes for Mollie, and to the
credential used for that request. Outside that window, recovery locates existing
provider objects rather than creating another purchase. A prepared attempt that
never reached provider submission can be closed even while the provider is down.
If the provider has not supplied an amount, the preview says it is unknown.

## Upgrade and recovery compatibility

A v0.3.13 Foghorn does not implement `TerminateStreamSessions`. Per-user stops
remain queued until that cell is upgraded; they never fall back to stopping the
whole tenant. New publishes are refused independently of this session-stop retry.

Platform-cluster `provision`, `upgrade`, `apply` and `diff` resolve the system
tenant through Quartermaster. A saved ID is used only for supported transport
recovery; Livepeer gateway clusters require a successful Quartermaster lookup.

`frameworks admin billing recovery upgrade --tenant-id <uuid> --upgrade-id <uuid>
--reason "Reconcile purchase"` previews a pending tier upgrade before asking for
confirmation. It cancels a request only after confirming it was not applied;
otherwise it completes the accepted purchase. It does not start another provider
purchase. Recovery retains the operator identity and reason, and reports the
actual durable state. Use `recovery list` for the purchase UUID and retry errors.

A suspended tenant's session-stop request is queued with its suspension. Partial
cell failures and Commodore outages retry durably. Resumption is checked under
the subscription lock before retrying, so a completed resumption clears the
queued stop without ending a new stream.
