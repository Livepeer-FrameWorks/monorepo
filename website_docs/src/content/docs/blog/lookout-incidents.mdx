An incident tracks the work that follows an alert: who owns it, what is still firing,
who acknowledged it, what changed, and whether the problem is actually over. FrameWorks already
had metrics and alert rules, but handing raw Alertmanager notifications to every tenant would have
mixed infrastructure details, tenant boundaries, and delivery policy into one brittle interface.

Lookout is the incident service between alerting and people. It receives grouped Alertmanager
notifications, resolves the alerting cluster to its current owner, and creates either a
platform-only incident or a tenant-visible incident. The result is available in the dashboard,
GraphQL, MCP tools, and realtime subscriptions.

## Tenant ownership is part of ingestion

Ownership is the hard part: cluster ownership can change while alerts are arriving, and a control-plane outage must never make another tenant's incident visible.

Lookout keeps a verified cluster-scope record from Quartermaster. Ingestion and ownership changes
coordinate through database locks and revisions, so an open incident is either written with the
new owner or moved to that owner after the concurrent ingest commits. When ownership cannot be
verified, the incident stays platform-scoped. That fails closed for tenant visibility while still
letting operators see an outage during a control-plane failure.

Resolved incidents do not move when ownership changes. They remain part of the operational
history of the tenant that owned the cluster when the incident happened.

## One incident, not a stream of pages

Alertmanager still does what it is good at: rule evaluation, grouping, inhibition, silences, and
delivery retries. Lookout turns each group into a state machine:

- new firing alerts open an incident;
- repeated notifications update the existing incident;
- acknowledgement records ownership without stopping alert ingestion;
- assignments and notes build an operational timeline;
- the incident auto-resolves when every alert resolves, or can be resolved manually;
- repeated copies of the same alert do not reopen a manually resolved incident.

Every state change is visible through `liveIncidentUpdates`, while GraphQL queries remain the
durable source of truth after a reconnect.

## Delivery survives the failure it reports

Platform incidents can notify email, Slack, and Discord. Tenant incidents are published for
Skipper investigation and tenant realtime delivery. Those sends use a lease-fenced transactional
outbox: the incident and its delivery obligation commit together, multiple Lookout replicas cannot
successfully settle the same claim, and temporary downstream failures retry with backoff.

Critical alerts also have a direct Alertmanager email route. That route deliberately bypasses
Lookout, so an outage in the incident service does not suppress the page reporting it. A permanent
Watchdog alert goes to an external heartbeat endpoint; if Alertmanager itself stops delivering,
the missing heartbeat becomes the signal.

## One workflow for people and agents

Tenants work incidents under **Infrastructure → Incidents**. Platform operators have a
cross-tenant view under **Platform Admin → Incidents**. The same actions are available through
GraphQL and the Gateway's MCP tools: list, inspect, acknowledge, assign, resolve, and add a note.

Skipper consumes the tenant incident feed and can attach an investigation report to the incident
timeline. It does not silently acknowledge or resolve anything; analysis and state-changing
operator actions remain separate.
